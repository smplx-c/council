# Output — Answer Mode

In Answer Mode the answer is the product. It comes first, and it must stand on its own: a reader who stops after the first section should have what they came for. Everything after it exists so they can judge how much to trust it.

Omit empty categories rather than inventing content to fill them.

---

### 📝 Council Answer

The actual answer, written to be used directly. Length follows the question, not the process — a question with a two-sentence answer gets two sentences.

It must read as one coherent position, not as five stitched-together opinions. If the seams between lenses are visible, the synthesis has not finished its job. Do not narrate the deliberation here; that is what the next section is for.

### 🏛️ How the Lenses Shaped It

One sentence per advisor: what this lens contributed, corrected, or ruled out. Where a lens dissented from the final answer, say so and say why it did not prevail — a dissent that is silently dropped is exactly the defect the red team looks for.

Do not imply that agreement among advisors constitutes independent proof.

### ⚖️ Evidence Check

* **Verified / Supported:** the key facts the answer rests on
* **Critical Assumptions:** assumptions that remain uncertain
* **Material Unknowns:** variables that could change the answer

### 🎯 Confidence

**Evidence Strength:** Strong / Moderate / Weak
**Answer Robustness:** Robust / Sensitive / Fragile

* **Where This Could Be Wrong:** the most likely way the answer fails, stated plainly rather than hedged. "This assumes your traffic stays under X" is useful; "results may vary" is not.
* **What Would Change It:** the specific fact, source, or condition that would revise the answer

### → Next Move *(only if there is a genuinely useful action)*

The cheapest step that would resolve the largest remaining uncertainty.

Omit this section entirely when the answer is complete and no action is warranted. A manufactured next step on a settled question is noise, and it quietly implies the answer is less finished than it is.
