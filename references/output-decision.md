# Output — Decision Mode

The verdict is the primary output. Everything above it exists to make the verdict inspectable; everything below it exists to make it actionable.

Omit empty categories rather than inventing content to fill them.

---

### 🏛️ Council Perspectives

Two to three sentences per advisor, each opening with its position.

* **Contrarian** — [POSITION] …
* **First Principles** — [POSITION] …
* **Expansionist** — [POSITION] …
* **Outsider** — [POSITION] …
* **Executor** — [POSITION] …

Do not imply that agreement among advisors constitutes independent proof. If four advisors agreed, that is four angles converging, not four witnesses.

### ⚖️ Evidence Check

* **Verified / Supported:** the key decision-relevant facts
* **Critical Assumptions:** important assumptions that remain uncertain
* **Material Unknowns:** unknown variables that could change the conclusion

### 👑 Verdict

**VERDICT:** STRONG YES / YES / LEAN YES / UNCLEAR / LEAN NO / NO / STRONG NO
**Evidence Strength:** Strong / Moderate / Weak
**Decision Robustness:** Robust / Sensitive / Fragile

* **Core Rationale:** the two or three arguments that should actually determine the decision — not a summary of everything said
* **Strongest Counterargument:** the best case against the verdict, stated fairly and without rhetorical weakening. If it reads as a straw man, it is one.
* **Tipping Point:** the specific fact, threshold, or condition most likely to reverse the verdict
* **Material Downside:** the most consequential plausible downside — if none is unusually important, say so plainly
* **Asymmetric Upside:** the most consequential plausible upside — if none is meaningful, say so plainly

### 📝 Council Answer *(only when the decision hinges on a substantive question the user also needs answered)*

The best available answer to that question, written to be used directly rather than as a summary of the deliberation. Keep it short — the verdict remains the primary output.

### → Next Move

The single action with the highest expected information or decision value. Prefer actions that test the core assumption, reduce uncertainty, cap downside, preserve optionality, and are cheap and reversible.

* **Do Tomorrow:** the concrete next action
* **Do NOT Do Yet:** the expensive, premature, or irreversible action the user is most likely to reach for first
