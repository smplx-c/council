# Output — Option Mode

Option Mode answers "which of these," not "yes or no." The choice is the primary output.

The ordering below is the synthesis's own reasoned ranking, derived from evidence and argument quality. It is never an aggregation of advisor preferences — counting how many lenses favored each option would be voting, which §1 forbids for good reason: the lens that spotted the disqualifying flaw is often outnumbered by four that did not look for it.

Omit empty categories rather than inventing content to fill them.

---

### 🏛️ Council Perspectives

Two to three sentences per advisor, each opening with its preferred option and margin.

* **Contrarian** — [OPTION, MARGIN] …
* **First Principles** — [OPTION, MARGIN] …
* **Expansionist** — [OPTION, MARGIN] …
* **Outsider** — [OPTION, MARGIN] …
* **Executor** — [OPTION, MARGIN] …

Where a lens found the options equivalent, say so — that is a finding about the comparison, not a missing answer.

### ⚖️ Evidence Check

* **Verified / Supported:** the facts that actually separate the options
* **Critical Assumptions:** assumptions the ranking depends on
* **Material Unknowns:** variables that could reorder the options

Focus on what *differentiates*. A fact equally true of every option cannot move the choice, however important it is in general.

### 👑 Choice

**CHOICE:** [option] — or NO CLEAR WINNER, or NONE OF THESE
**Margin:** Decisive / Narrow / Toss-up
**Evidence Strength:** Strong / Moderate / Weak
**Choice Robustness:** Robust / Sensitive / Fragile

* **Core Rationale:** the two or three arguments that should actually decide it
* **What the Winner Costs:** what the user gives up by choosing it. Every option trades something away; a choice presented as pure gain has not been analyzed.
* **Ranking of the Rest:** the remaining options in order, one line each on why it lost — the specific deficit, not a general impression
* **Switching Condition:** the specific fact, threshold, or change under which the runner-up becomes the right choice. This is the most valuable line in the output: it tells the user what to watch after deciding.
* **Disqualified:** any option ruled out outright, and on what grounds — omit if none
* **Missing Option:** an option absent from the user's list that the analysis suggests belongs on it, if any. Say plainly if the strongest move is one the user did not propose; a well-run comparison between wrong options is worse than useless, because its rigor makes the result feel earned.

**Toss-up handling.** If two options are genuinely close, say so rather than inventing a tiebreaker. Then name the cheapest thing that would break the tie — and if nothing would, say that too and recommend choosing on secondary criteria (reversibility, speed, team familiarity), explicitly labelled as such.

### → Next Move

* **Do Tomorrow:** the concrete next action, usually the cheapest step that tests the switching condition
* **Do NOT Do Yet:** the expensive or irreversible commitment the choice does not yet justify
